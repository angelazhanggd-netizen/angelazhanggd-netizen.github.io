Section 01

Project Overview

A rigorous feasibility study and full-process simulation of a hydrometallurgical plant designed to recover lithium, cobalt, and nickel from end-of-life lithium-ion battery packs at industrial scale.

Background & Context

Australia's battery storage capacity is projected to grow 15× by 2030, generating an estimated 135,000 tonnes of spent battery waste annually. Current landfill disposal leaches toxic heavy metals into groundwater and forfeits critical minerals worth AUD $4.2B per year. Hydrometallurgical processing offers a thermodynamically efficient, low-temperature alternative that recovers >98% of valuable metals while meeting EPA waste discharge standards — directly supporting Australia's Critical Minerals Strategy 2030.

Project Objectives

  • ▸ Design a 6-stage hydrometallurgical flowsheet: pretreatment → leaching → purification → solvent extraction → stripping → precipitation
  • ▸ Develop a full Aspen Plus process simulation to validate mass and energy balances under realistic operating conditions
  • ▸ Produce AutoCAD PFD and P&ID documentation to engineering-deliverable standard
  • ▸ Specify and size all major process equipment with material selection and HAZOP-ready design
  • ▸ Demonstrate CO₂ and energy intensity reductions vs pyrometallurgical baseline

Key Design Considerations

1
Acid Stoichiometry Control — H₂SO₄:battery mass ratio maintained at 1.5:1 mol/mol to maximise metal dissolution while minimising reagent waste and downstream neutralisation load.
2
pH-Staged Precipitation — Sequential pH adjustment (pH 3.5 → 5.5 → 10.5) selectively precipitates Fe/Al impurities before Co/Ni/Li separation, eliminating cross-contamination of product streams.
3
Heat Integration — Pinch analysis identified 3 process-to-process heat exchangers, reducing utility demand by 31% versus unintegrated design. Hot leachate effluent pre-heats incoming feed slurry.
4
Closed-Loop Reagent Recycling — Spent organic extractant (D2EHPA / Cyanex 272) from the stripping stage is regenerated and recirculated, reducing solvent makeup to <2% of total inventory per cycle.
5
Effluent Treatment — Acidic bleed streams are treated via lime neutralisation and constructed wetland polishing before discharge, meeting the Environment Protection (Water) Policy 2015.

Section 03

Aspen Plus Mass & Energy Balance

Rigorous steady-state simulation using Aspen Plus V14 with the ELECNRTL thermodynamic property package, validated against published pilot-plant data (±3% deviation).

Simulation Methodology

The simulation employs the ELECNRTL electrolyte property method to accurately model ion activity in concentrated H₂SO₄/H₂O leach solutions. Acid leaching kinetics were implemented via a Langmuir–Hinshelwood rate expression fitted to experimental dissolution data at 60–80 °C. Convergence was achieved via Aspen's SM (Successive Modular) algorithm with a convergence tolerance of 10⁻⁵ on mole balance. Heat integration was subsequently optimised using Aspen Energy Analyzer, identifying 3 pinch matches saving 31% on utility load.

Conclusion & Technical Highlights

This project demonstrates end-to-end process engineering capability — from thermodynamic modelling and simulation to production-ready equipment specification.

"A fully validated, simulation-backed hydrometallurgical flowsheet that recovers lithium, cobalt, and nickel from end-of-life batteries at >97% efficiency — at 42% lower carbon intensity than conventional smelting, with a projected payback period under 5 years at 2.4 t/h feed scale."

Innovation & Technical Breakthroughs

Selective Leaching at Ambient Pressure

Achieved 98.3% Li extraction at atmospheric pressure using H₂SO₄ + H₂O₂ reductant at 70 °C — eliminating autoclave requirements and cutting CapEx by ~22% vs. pressure leach alternatives.

Multi-Component SX Cascade

Sequenced D2EHPA / Cyanex 272 extraction achieving simultaneous Co/Ni/Mn separation in a single 5-stage battery with >99% selectivity — a design validated against published McCabe-Thiele stagewise calculations.

Heat Pinch Integration

Aspen Energy Analyzer pinch study identified ΔT_min = 10 °C minimum approach, enabling 3 cross-heat exchangers that eliminate one LP steam generation unit and reduce cooling tower duty by 28%.

Closed-Loop Reagent Circuit

Organic solvent regeneration cycle reduces D2EHPA makeup to <2% per pass, cutting annual operating costs by ~AUD $340K/year at design throughput.
